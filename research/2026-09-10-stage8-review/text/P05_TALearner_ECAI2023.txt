Proposition 2. Let M be a labelled MDP. For any two TAs A , A ′ , if L(A ) = L(A ′ ), then M ⊗ A ∼
                                                                                                 = M ⊗ A ′.

Proof. Consider the two product Markov processes M ⊗ A and M ⊗ A ′ constructed by composing the MDP M with each TA A and A ′ .
At each time step t, the agent’s observation ot is a pair in S × {0, 1}. Therefore, observation sequences restricted to the first component of the
observation pair are state trajectories, and restricted to the second component are reward sequences (χF (qt ))τt=0 ∈ {0, 1}∗ . The probabilities
of state trajectories must be equal for M ⊗ A and M ⊗ A ′ since they are constructed from identical environments M, and the probabilities
of reward sequences must be equal because the languages of A and A are identical by assumption.


Proposition 3. Suppose an agent is given a structurally correct product MDP M̂ ⊗ Aˆ belonging to [M ⊗ A ], where M is the true MDP
underlying the product MDP, and the agent can observe s and q for all ⟨s, q⟩ ∈ Ŝ ⊗ . Then, the agent can learn the MDP-restricted TA AˆM̂ ,
but may not be able to learn the full TA Aˆ.

Proof. Start with the product MDP M̂ ⊗ Aˆ, where the agent can observe the MDP and TA state components (s and q) of each product MDP
state s⊗ = ⟨s, q⟩. Induce a product MC M̂π ⊗ Aˆ by employing a random exploration policy π. Note that any exploration policy can be used
here, provided that every action is chosen at every state with non-zero probability. For each transition P ⊗ (⟨sj , qj ⟩ | ⟨si , qi ⟩) > 0 in M̂π ⊗ Aˆ,

not be equal to the true underlying TA A because there might exist a path in A that corresponds with a trace that cannot be generated by an

Proposition 4. Given a labelled product MC Mπ ⊗ A with binary reward, labelling every edge ⟨si , q⟩ → ⟨sj , qj ⟩ according to the label of
its target state’s MDP component (i.e., in this case, L(sj ) = ℓj ), results in an NFA.

Proof. An NFA is a tuple (Q, q0 , Σ, δ, F) (Definition 2). The set of states Q is the set of states of the product MDP. The state q0 is the same
as the initial state of the product MDP (since the initial states of the MDP M and TA A are known). Σ = 2AP since these are the possible
labels of the edges. δ is indeed a non-deterministic transition function since from any state s ∈ S, there may be multiple si ∈ S such that a
transition from s to si is possible, and L(si ) = ℓ. That is, multiple possible next states may exist, which all have the same label. Finally, the
accepting states F are the accepting states of the product MC Mπ ⊗ A – i.e., the states where the reward observed is non-zero.


Proposition 5. Let N be the NFA underlying the learnt product MC Mπ ⊗ A . Then, L(N ) = L(AM ), where AM is the MDP-restricted
true TA.

Proof. N accepts precisely those words that terminate at an accept state of the product MC Mπ ⊗ A , and because a product state is an accept
state if and only if its TA-component is accepting, the language recognised by N must be contained in the language of the true underlying TA.
Moreover, because the set of paths in N underlying Mπ ⊗ A is precisely the set of MDP-attainable paths of the true TA, the language of N
is precisely the language of the MDP-restricted TA AM .

F    Comparisons with Related work
We attempted to benchmark our algorithm against several existing passive DFA learning libraries. Two well-established and commonly-used
approaches to this are the RPNI algorithm (used in, e.g., [36, 37]) and SAT-based algorithms (used in e.g., [1, 8, 15, 34, 36]). The results of our
comparisons are summarised in Table 2. Of the six implementations we tried, only the SAT-based approach from the libalf library was able to

RPNI algorithm indicating its better efficiency compared to existing approaches. Additionally, most of the libraries we attempted to run using
